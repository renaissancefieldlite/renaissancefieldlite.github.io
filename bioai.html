Nesting 1–5

Five connected lanes. Each keeps its own measurements, tools, and validation rules.

The Nesting map gives BioAI cross-scale context without flattening disciplines into one schema. In the proposed biological tests, the five functions govern leakage checks, context, evidence decisions, and routing while samples, labels, and models remain lane-specific.

N1 Formal systems

Identity, transformation, timing, controls, nulls, and the logical rules that determine whether a relation may enter the supported state.

N2 Matter + chemistry

Material, molecular, morphology, composition, and laboratory observations retain their native source and measurement boundaries.

N3 Fields + physical cycles

Energy, environment, waves, periodicity, and physical context become explicit state inputs rather than detached background metadata.

N4 Biology + physiology

Biosignals, images, cells, tissue, interventions, and outcomes are organized by scale, time, quality, recurrence, and evidentiary role.

N5 Convergence + evidence memory

Supported relations converge into traceable memory, reviewer decisions, failure routes, and research-product workflows without promoting open hypotheses into facts.

Current component support

The integrated hypothesis is open. The underlying state, capture, routing, and control work is real.

Measured physiology

Repeated state capture

Fifteen valid repeated condition rows carried target, calm-control, and drift-control states across 98,027 Muse packet rows, 9,750,684 decoded samples, and 3,980 HRV observations. The strongest current support is HRV separation; other lanes remain quality bounded.

Runtime routing

State and continuity stay visible

RFL normalized 468 recorded state-frame rows and reconciled 480 expected source, guard, and sink events across eight named lane sinks with zero observed drops and zero sequence gaps.

Real-data controls

Different biological data types

Cell-morphology and clinical/metabolic benchmark surfaces were run against shuffled-label and feature-shuffle controls, establishing reusable packet, control, and receipt discipline across distinct biological inputs.

Support boundary

Components, not a pre-claimed outcome

These receipts reduce implementation risk. They do not establish the proposed integrated BioAI advantage, population validation, diagnostic utility, or clinical performance.

Proposed Phase I validation

Two products. Three test surfaces. One shared evidence architecture.

The bounded NSF design tests how the larger architecture behaves in biology. Human physiology and veterinary imagery remain independent populations with separate labels, models, splits, and domain rules. No human–dog correspondence or sample-level fusion is assumed.

Lane A1 / B.A.S.I.S.

Protocol-specific state transitions

PhysioNet stress and structured-exercise data test stress/rest, aerobic baseline-work-cooldown-recovery, and anaerobic sprint-recovery tasks without inventing cross-protocol subject continuity.

Lane A2 / B.A.S.I.S.

Degradation + redundant evidence

ScientISST MOVE tests masking, corruption, motion, missing channels, and alternate routing while paired sensors remain source-distinct rather than interchangeable ground truth.

Lane B / Quanta Vision

Veterinary image robustness

A permissively licensed 95-dog dataset uses dog-grouped splits for classification, image degradation, calibration, and evidence-trace tests inside the veterinary boundary.

Shared architecture test

Nesting functions without false fusion

Across all three evaluations, Nests 1–5 govern leakage checks, context, evidence decisions, and routing while samples, labels, models, and field rules remain separate.

Prospective gates

Clean-data noninferiority; at least 80% retention under the primary degradation condition; calibrated uncertainty; 100% output-to-source traceability; zero silent imputation; unsupported relations fail closed; and reproducible evidence manifests. Predictive superiority remains a stretch target.
